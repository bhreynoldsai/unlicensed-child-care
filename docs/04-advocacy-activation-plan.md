**TRUE NORTH STRATEGIES**

**Advocacy Activation Plan**

*Turning the supporter base into legislator contacts, testimony, and
district pressure*

Georgia Child Care Grassroots Activation Initiative \| August 2026 \|
Internal Working Document — Draft for Discussion

1\. Strategy

Legislators respond to identifiable constituents in their own district,
at volume, on a live vote. The platform exists to produce exactly that:
when a bill or rule affecting license-exempt programs moves, every
supporter in a relevant district receives a role-appropriate ask matched
to their own Representative and Senator, within hours. The message frame
is affirmative — every child in care deserves the same health and safety
protections, and every provider deserves the same rules — not an attack
on churches, camps, or recreation programs.

2\. Role-Based Activation Ladder

Ask size scales with role. Each segment's default ask is the largest one
they will reliably complete:

| **Segment**                                    | **Default ask**                                      | **Escalation ask**                             | **Voice/frame**                                                                |
|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Teacher                                        | One-click pre-drafted email to their legislators     | Short call using provided script               | “I meet these standards every day — every program caring for children should.” |
| Other (support staff, cooks, drivers, parents) | One-click email; share link with coworkers           | Call                                           | Community member who sees licensed care up close                               |
| Corporate staff director / Director            | Email + call; recruit their center's staff           | Written committee testimony                    | Operational expert: what licensure actually requires                           |
| Regional manager                               | Calls to legislators across their centers' districts | In-person testimony; legislator center visits  | Multi-community employer perspective                                           |
| Owner                                          | Direct legislator relationship; testimony            | Host a center tour for their legislator; op-ed | Job creator and safety investor in the district                                |

3\. Campaign Triggers and Calendar

- Session calendar: Georgia General Assembly convenes January–early
  April; Crossover Day is the hard deadline for a bill to clear its
  origin chamber — alert tempo peaks in the two weeks before it.

- Triggers: bill introduction or amendment touching licensure
  exemptions; DECAL rulemaking notices and comment windows; committee
  hearing notices (often \<48 hours — the reason the platform must
  activate same-day).

- Off-session: interim study committees, legislator center visits, and
  district density reporting to keep the network warm.

4\. Campaign Mechanics

- Alert → action page: each alert links to a page pre-filled with the
  supporter's matched legislators, an editable draft message, and (for
  calls) a script and the office number.

- Message drafts differ by role — a teacher's email should not read like
  an owner's.

- Every action is logged to the supporter record, powering
  activation-rate metrics and identifying the high-engagement supporters
  who become testimony candidates.

5\. District Density as a Lobbying Asset

Before each session, produce a one-page report per target legislator:
number of supporters in their district by role, centers operating there,
children served, and jobs represented. The lobbying team carries these
into meetings; the grassroots layer and the direct-lobbying layer
reference each other.

6\. Messaging Discipline

- Lead with child safety parity: ratios, background checks, training,
  inspections — protections children in exempt programs do not get.

- Second frame, for business audiences: level playing field — licensed
  operators carry compliance costs their exempt competitors do not.

- Never: attacks on faith-based organizations, camps, or rec leagues as
  institutions. The target is the exemption policy, not the programs'
  communities — many legislators belong to them.

7\. Metrics per Campaign

| **Metric**          | **Definition**                                                         |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reach               | Supporters alerted in targeted districts                               |
| Activation rate     | Actions completed ÷ alerts delivered (benchmark 10–20% for email asks) |
| Legislator coverage | Share of target committee members receiving 10+ constituent contacts   |
| Escalations         | Calls made, testimony delivered, center visits hosted                  |
